LTV is the number that decides how aggressively you can afford to buy customers. If a customer contributes ₹2,592 in gross profit and costs ₹1,200 to acquire, you can scale confidently. If they contribute ₹900, paid acquisition at that CAC is destroying value however good the ROAS looks.

The discipline that keeps LTV honest is to build it from gross profit, not revenue, and from observed repeat behaviour rather than an assumed lifetime. A young brand simply does not know its true LTV yet, and a modelled figure stretched over three years is a way of justifying a CAC you cannot currently afford.

For most Indian D2C categories we would rather look at a 90-day and a 180-day value than a lifetime one. Those windows are measurable, they map to how quickly cash comes back, and they are hard to argue with. If the 90-day value already covers CAC, the brand can scale on its own cash. If it does not, growth is being funded from somewhere else, and that is a decision the founder should make deliberately.
