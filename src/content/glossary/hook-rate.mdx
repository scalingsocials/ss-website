Hook rate isolates the first three seconds, which is where most video ads are lost. It is a useful diagnostic precisely because it separates two failures that look identical in the top-line numbers: nobody watched, versus they watched and did not care.

If hook rate is low, the problem is the opening frame — it is not stopping the scroll. That is usually fixable without reshooting anything: a different first second, an on-screen line that names the product immediately, a tighter crop. If hook rate is healthy but conversions are not, the problem is further in, in what the ad argues or what the landing page does next.

The judgement to keep is that a hook exists to earn attention for something worth seeing. On premium products especially, an opening that shows the product properly usually outperforms a clever misdirect, because the people it stops are the people who might actually buy. Attention you cannot convert is a cost, not a win.
