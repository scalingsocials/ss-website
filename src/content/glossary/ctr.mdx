CTR is the clearest early signal that an ad is talking to the right person. It is available long before you have enough purchases to judge anything statistically, which makes it genuinely useful in the first days of a test.

It is also easy to game in ways that hurt you. An ad that withholds what the product is will earn clicks from curiosity, and those visitors leave immediately. We would rather run a slightly lower CTR with creative that shows the product honestly, because the traffic it sends actually converts. On premium-priced products this is not a marginal difference: showing drape, fit and finish properly is usually what separates a campaign that returns from one that does not.

Read CTR as a diagnostic, never as a target. Rising CTR with flat conversions means you are attracting the wrong attention. Falling CTR on an ad that has been live for weeks is fatigue, not failure. Steady CTR with steady conversions is the boring, profitable state you are aiming for.
