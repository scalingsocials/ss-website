Most creative briefs optimise for the wrong horizon. They ask for something that will perform, which in practice means something that will perform in week one — and week-one performance and durability are close to unrelated.

The cost of that mistake is hidden in the rebuild. Every time a winner burns out and the campaign is reconstructed, the learning phase resets, accumulated signal is discarded, and the account pays again for answers it already had.

## Why surprise fatigues and demonstration does not

A hook built on surprise works exactly once per viewer. By the third exposure the surprise is gone and all that remains is the ad.

A hook built on demonstration behaves differently, because the information is still useful on the fifth viewing. Someone considering a ₹6,000 garment wants to see drape, movement, fabric and fit, and seeing it again does not diminish the value of seeing it.

This is why, on premium products, the creative that shows the product properly tends to outlast the clever misdirect — and usually converts better while it is running, because the attention it earns is qualified.

## What to put in the brief

**The variable you are testing.** One per concept. A batch of ads that differ in six ways teaches you nothing when one wins.

**The angle, not the execution.** "Show the garment in motion so the drape is obvious" is a brief. "Make it feel premium" is a mood.

**Whether it has to survive repetition.** If this is intended as a scaling asset rather than a seasonal push, say so, because it changes what the opening should do.

**The price point.** Consideration changes everything about what the ad has to accomplish. At ₹800 you need a scroll-stop. At ₹6,000 you need to answer hesitation.

## Judge results on the right axis

When a test concludes, most teams rank on peak performance. Rank on decay instead: which ads were still working in week four, and at what frequency.

We have run a kidswear account where the winning campaign stayed live for eight months, through a full seasonal cycle, without a rebuild. Nothing about that is exciting to report. It is also considerably cheaper than a quarterly rebuild cycle, and it let budget go into scaling a known asset rather than re-discovering one.

## Read fatigue correctly when it does arrive

Everything fatigues eventually. The signature is specific: click-through falling while frequency rises and cost per click climbs, with targeting and offer unchanged.

The response is a genuinely new angle into the existing structure — a different argument, format or hook. Not a recolour of the same idea, which fatigues again almost immediately, and not a campaign rebuild, which solves a creative problem by throwing away account learning.
