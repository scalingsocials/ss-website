# Search Console baseline — 13 September 2026

Captured the evening **before** the WordPress → Astro cutover, while the old
Hostinger site was still live and ranking. This is the reference to diff against
after launch.

- **Property:** `https://scalingsocials.com/` (URL-prefix property, verified as `support@scalingsocials.com`)
- **Source:** Google Search Console via the Supermetrics connector
- **Setting:** `final_data = true` — finalised rows only, so the 2–3 day
  reporting lag cannot understate the baseline

**How to read it later.** A 10–20% organic dip for two to four weeks is normal
for a migration. A 50% drop means something is broken, and redirects are the
first place to look. Compare the **28-day totals** and the **top pages** table
first — those move fastest when a redirect is wrong.

**Read clicks with care.** Most clicks here are branded and navigational (people
sent a link on a sales call, who then search the name), so click volume partly
tracks sales activity rather than search performance. **Impressions and average
position are the honest migration signals.** See the analysis at the end.

## What this baseline says about the migration

**Brand clicks are navigational, not discovery — owner-confirmed.** `scaling
socials` and variants are ~76% of 28-day clicks at position 2.2, but these are
overwhelmingly people who were *already told about the company* — links shared on
sales calls, then the name typed into Google. They are not organic acquisition.

Two consequences, and they cut in opposite directions:

- **Brand CLICK volume is not an SEO health signal.** It tracks sales activity.
  A quiet fortnight of calls will drop brand clicks with nothing wrong on the
  site. Do not read it as migration damage.
- **Brand POSITION still is a signal.** If the site stops ranking 1–2 for its own
  name, something is genuinely broken. Watch the position column, not the clicks.

**Organic acquisition is close to zero today, which lowers the migration risk.**
`performance marketing agency bangalore` drew 603 impressions in 90 days for
**1 click** at position 35.7. Strip out brand and navigational traffic and the
old site acquires almost nobody from search. There is very little to lose and a
large pool of commercial demand sitting on page 4 — that is the headroom the new
site is built for. Judge recovery on **impressions and average position**, not
clicks.

**`/careers/` — Meta ads point at it, and after cutover it 301s off-domain** to
`careers.scalingsocials.com`. Owner-confirmed context, which settles most of the
concern:

- **No Google Ads run at all**, so the cross-domain "destination mismatch"
  disapproval risk does not apply. Meta tolerates redirects.
- **The different Meta pixel on the careers site is deliberate** — job seekers
  and D2C founders are different audiences and are kept apart on purpose. Not a
  misconfiguration, and the absence of GA4 there is consistent with that.

What is left is smaller but real:

1. **Query strings must survive the 301**, or `fbclid` never reaches the careers
   pixel and paid landings stop attributing. Cloudflare Pages forwards query
   strings on `_redirects` rules, but this has not been tested on this site —
   verify it at cutover as part of the redirect spot-check.
2. **The extra hop costs latency** on a mobile ad click, and some Meta
   link-click → landing-page-view attribution is lost on any redirect.
3. **Equity** transfers less reliably cross-domain, on 18 clicks / 427
   impressions of organic.

**Simplest fix, low urgency: point the Meta ad destination straight at
`https://careers.scalingsocials.com/`.** No redirect, no query-string question,
no lost hop. The 301 stays in `_redirects` for organic traffic and old links
regardless.

**`/performance-marketing-agency-bangalore/` is the one to protect.** 1,210
impressions in 28 days, the highest of any page, and it keeps its URL. Any change
to its title, H1 or content is a live experiment on your biggest impression pool.
